High-Performance Actuator R&D & Kinematic System Design

Techligence Pvt. Ltd | May 2026 – July 2026

During my two-month internship at Techligence Pvt. Ltd., I took full ownership of the mechanical design and rapid iteration of custom high-performance actuators for a 7+ DOF anthropomorphic arm, alongside developing structural subsystems for an AI receptionist robot. Working strictly from first principles and academic literature, I analytically designed, CAD-modeled, and evaluated three distinct actuator architectures to handle complex kinematic loads. This included establishing a baseline 50:1 harmonic drive, engineering a dual-stage 49:1 cycloidal actuator to manage severe moment loads at the proximal joints, and developing a novel custom harmonic drive featuring an axially compressed, deflection-isolated flexspline. Concurrently, I drove the design-to-validation cycle for the receptionist robot's mechanical assemblies to ensure robust deployment reliability.

Note: Due to strict Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDA), full proprietary subsystem layouts and specific integration schematics are restricted from public display. The mechanical architectures detailed below focus strictly on my localized actuator R&D and subsystem verification workflows.

AI Receptionist Robot Subsystems

Managed the end-to-end mechanical engineering lifecycle for structural subsystems intended for customer-facing deployment.

  • Design-to-Validation Cycle: Supervised full physical synthesis including detailed CAD creation, physical prototyping, and empirical testing under expected service envelopes.
  • Precision Manufacturing: Executed meticulous linear and radial tolerance stack-up analyses to mitigate assembly interferences and guide precision CNC machining and manufacturing parameters.

Literature-Derived Cycloidal Architecture (49:1)

Engineered a highly specialized, dual-stage cycloidal actuator architecture based directly on mathematical formulations from contemporary mechanical research papers. Designed exclusively for high-load proximal joints to manage and distribute severe moment loads across the assembly envelope.

  • Kinematic Ratio: Achieved a precise 49:1 reduction ratio by implementing a 7-lobe primary stage feeding into a 6-lobe secondary stage.

Advanced Custom Flexspline Prototype

Drove the structural design through successive iterations utilizing radial deflection limits and bending stress analysis as absolute primary constraints.

  • Envelope Optimization: Engineered a custom flexspline cross-section that achieves high "cup-style" geometric efficiency within a significantly reduced axial envelope.
